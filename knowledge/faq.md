Answers are short and consistent with the topic files. Refer to injuries.md for any pain question beyond the basics.

## Why do I have to run so slowly on easy days?
Easy running builds the aerobic base (capillaries, mitochondria) and lets tendons and bones adapt without adding fatigue. ~80% of running should be easy so the 20% hard sessions can be done well. [Seiler 2010] Use the talk test: full sentences.

## My heart rate is very high even when I jog slowly. What is wrong?
Usually nothing: heat, humidity, caffeine, poor sleep, dehydration, stress and low fitness all raise HR. Wrist sensors also misread (cadence lock). HRmax formulas are ±10-12 bpm off for individuals. [Tanaka 2001] Go by the talk test; HR drops at the same pace over 6-12 weeks of consistent easy running. Chest pain, dizziness or palpitations: stop and see a doctor.

## How do I get faster?
Consistency and volume first (more easy running over months), then 1-2 quality sessions per week (threshold and intervals at VDOT paces), plus strides. Most amateurs improve fastest by running more easy km, not more hard sessions. [Daniels]

## How much should I increase my weekly distance?
~5-10% per week (less with age: ~8% from 50, ~5-7% from 60; never more than 20%), with a lighter week every 3rd-4th week. More important: never make a single run > 10% longer than your longest run in the last 30 days. [Frandsen 2025; Nielsen 2014]

## Is it OK to run every day?
Yes for experienced runners with mostly easy days. Beginners: rest day between runs for the first 2-3 months. Frequency grows only as tissues adapt.

## Can I run two hard days in a row?
Not recommended for most amateurs; leave at least one easy or rest day between quality sessions and around the long run. [methodology.md]

## What is a tempo run and how fast should it be?
Continuous ~20 min (up to 40 min) at threshold pace: "comfortably hard", a pace you could race for about 1 hour. RPE 6-7. Not 10K race effort. [Daniels; Pfitzinger]

## What are strides?
4-8 x 15-25 s smooth accelerations to about mile pace, full recovery, after an easy run. They improve form and economy with almost no fatigue. Fine for everyone after the first month of continuous running.

## Do hills replace speedwork?
Partly. Hill repeats (60-90 s at 5K effort) give strength and VO2max stimulus with less impact; short hill sprints build power. You still need some flat race-pace work in the final 4-6 weeks before a race. [Daniels; Pfitzinger]

## Treadmill vs outside, does it count the same?
Yes. Set 1% incline to match outdoor effort. [Jones & Doust 1996] Treadmill pace can feel harder or easier; go by effort. Fine for bad weather, darkness, or controlled intervals.

## I only have 30 minutes. What should I do?
30 min easy (most days). If it is a quality day: 10 min warm-up + 3 x 4 min at threshold with 1 min jog + 5 min cool-down, or 10 min warm-up + 6 x 1 min fast / 1 min easy + cool-down.

## How many kilometres per week do I need for a marathon?
To start a marathon plan: ≥ 30-40 km/week consistently. Plans peak at ~55-70 km (beginner/Hansons beginner), 70-90 km (intermediate), 90+ km (advanced). Finishing is possible on less, but the last 10 km suffer. [Pfitzinger; Hansons]

## How long should my longest run be before a marathon?
~29-32 km for most runners (Pfitzinger), or ~26 km with higher weekly volume (Hansons) — but never more than ~3 h, whichever comes first; beyond that, recovery cost outweighs benefit. Slower runners reach 3 h before 29 km, and that is enough. Peak long run 3-5 weeks before race.

## What pace should my long run be?
Easy pace (E), often 60-90 s/km slower than marathon pace. Experienced runners can add M-pace segments in the last part during the build. [Daniels; Pfitzinger]

## Should I use heart rate or pace?
Easy runs: effort or HR. Threshold and marathon sessions: pace, with effort as check in heat/hills. Intervals and reps: pace (HR lags). [Daniels]

## Is my watch's VO2max / race predictor accurate?
It is an estimate, often off by several %. A recent race result gives a more reliable VDOT. Use the watch trend, not the number.

## I missed a week. Do I repeat it or carry on?
Missed ≤ 1 week (not injured/ill): continue from where the plan is now, first 2-3 runs easy. Do not double up to catch up. Fitness loss after one week is negligible. [Mujika & Padilla 2000]

## How much fitness do I lose if I stop?
Very little in 1-2 weeks. VO2max drops noticeably after ~2-4 weeks off (roughly 4-14% over 4 weeks). It returns faster than it was built. [Mujika & Padilla 2000]

## Should I run if I feel a bit sick?
"Neck check": symptoms only above the neck (runny nose, mild sore throat) -> easy short run is OK if you feel fine. Symptoms below the neck (fever, chest congestion, body aches, vomiting/diarrhoea) -> no running. Never run with fever (heart muscle inflammation risk). Resume easy running 1-2 days after the fever is gone, then build gradually over as many days as you were ill.

## My legs feel heavy. Rest or run easy?
Normal muscle heaviness after hard training: run easy and short, or rest. If heaviness lasts > 1 week with poor sleep, high resting HR, and slower paces at usual effort: take 2-3 easy/rest days and reduce the week. Persistent fatigue > 2-3 weeks: see a doctor (iron, under-fuelling, illness).

## Can I run with sore muscles (DOMS)?
Yes, easy and short; general muscle soreness 24-72 h after a hard session or race is normal. Sharp, one-sided, joint or bone pain is not DOMS; follow injuries.md.

## I get side stitches. What helps?
Slow down, breathe out deeply, press the painful spot, bend slightly forward. Avoid big meals and lots of fluid in the 2 h before running. Stitches become less frequent as fitness improves.

## How should I breathe?
Through mouth and nose together, whatever feels natural. No need to force a pattern. If you cannot speak in sentences on easy runs, you are going too fast.

## Should I change from heel striking to forefoot?
No need. Foot strike itself has not been shown to reduce injury, and forcing a change shifts load to the calf and Achilles. More useful: avoid overstriding; +5-10% cadence reduces knee and hip load. [Heiderscheit 2011]

## Do I need a cadence of 180?
No. 180 was an observation of elite racers. Cadence depends on height and speed. Increasing your own cadence by 5-10% is a valid tool for knee pain or overstriding. [Heiderscheit 2011]

## Do I need to stretch before running?
Static stretching before running is not needed and long holds (> 60 s) may reduce performance slightly. Use a dynamic warm-up (easy jog, leg swings, drills). Stretch after if you like it; it has little effect on injury risk. [Behm 2016]

## Does strength training help runners?
Yes. Strength training reduced sports injuries to about one-third in a meta-analysis, and heavy/plyometric training improves running economy. 2x/week, 20-30 min. [Lauersen 2014]

## My knee / shin / heel hurts. Can I keep running?
Pain 0-2/10 that does not change your stride: continue, monitor. 3-5/10: reduce (shorter, slower, flatter) and check it settles in 24 h. ≥ 6/10, pain that worsens during the run, sharp bone pain, or limping: stop and get it assessed. [injuries.md]

## I have black toenails and blisters. What to do?
Usually shoes too small or loose lacing on downhills. Choose a thumb's width of space in front of the longest toe, lock-lace the heel, use synthetic/merino socks and anti-chafe. Signs of infection (redness spreading, pus, heat): doctor.

## Which running shoes should I buy?
Choose the most comfortable shoe for you in a running shop; comfort predicts fewer injuries better than foot type. "Pronation control" matching has weak evidence. [Nielsen 2015] Replace when worn or when new aches appear (often 500-800 km).

## Are carbon plated "super shoes" worth it?
They improve running economy by roughly 2-4% on average in studies [Hoogkamer 2018], most useful for races and fast sessions. Test them in training first; some runners report calf/Achilles or foot strain. Not necessary for easy running.

## Should I eat before a morning run?
Easy run < 60 min: optional, fasted is fine for most. Quality session or long run: small carb snack 30-60 min before (banana, toast with honey) or a bigger meal 2-3 h before. [Thomas 2016]

## When do I need gels?
Runs over ~75-90 min: 30-60 g carbs per hour; > 2.5 h up to 60-90 g/h if practised. Start at 30-45 min. Practise in long runs first. [Thomas 2016]

## How much should I drink during a run?
Drink to thirst on most runs. Long/hot runs: typical 400-800 ml per hour with sodium; avoid losing > 2% body weight and never gain weight during a race. [Sawka 2007]

## Does coffee help performance?
Yes: 3-6 mg/kg caffeine ~60 min before improves endurance for most; lower doses also work. Test in training first. [Guest 2021]

## Can running help me lose weight?
Running increases energy use and supports health, but weight change mostly depends on overall diet. Undereating while training raises injury and illness risk. The coach does not set weight or calorie targets; for weight goals, a doctor or dietitian can help. [nutrition.md]

## How should I pace my first half marathon?
Goal from a recent race (VDOT) or conservative estimate. Run the first 5 km 3-5 s/km slower than goal pace, hold goal pace to 16 km, then speed up if able. Even pacing beats a fast start. [race-prep.md]

## Is it OK to walk during a marathon?
Yes. Planned walk breaks (e.g. 1 min every 1-2 km or at aid stations) help many first-time and slower marathoners finish stronger. Unplanned walking after starting too fast is the usual problem.

## Why do I feel sluggish during my taper?
Normal. Reduced volume causes restlessness, phantom aches and "flat" legs. Fitness does not drop in 2-3 weeks; the taper brings ~2-3% performance gain. Do not add workouts. [Bosquet 2007]

## How long should I rest after a marathon?
1-2 weeks of rest or very easy running, no hard sessions for ~3-4 weeks. Half marathon: ~1 easy week. 5K/10K: 2-4 easy days. [Pfitzinger]
