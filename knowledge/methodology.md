## 1. Intensity zones (3-zone model used across all files)
- Zone 1 (easy): below first ventilatory/lactate threshold (VT1/LT1, ~2 mmol/L lactate). Conversational. ~<80% HRmax. = Daniels E pace. [Seiler 2010]
- Zone 2 (moderate): between VT1 and VT2. "Comfortably hard". ~80-90% HRmax. = Daniels M and T pace.
- Zone 3 (hard): above VT2/LT2 (~4 mmol/L). ~>90% HRmax. = Daniels I and R pace.
- Map to VDOT paces in vdot.md.

## 2. 80/20 (polarized / pyramidal) intensity distribution
- Elite endurance athletes do ~75-80% of sessions in Zone 1, the rest at Zone 2-3. [Seiler & Kjerland 2006; Seiler 2010]
- Sub-elite runners doing ~80% Zone 1 improved 10.4 km cross-country times more than those doing ~65% Zone 1 at equal total load. [Esteve-Lanao 2007]
- A 9-week polarized block (≈80/0/20) improved VO2max and time to exhaustion more than threshold-heavy, HIIT-only, or high-volume-only programmes in trained athletes. [Stöggl & Sperlich 2014]
- Pyramidal (Z1 > Z2 > Z3) is also well supported, especially for marathoners (lots of M/T work). Both share the core rule: most running is easy. [Seiler 2010]
- Apply as: count by SESSIONS. Of 5 runs/week -> 1-2 quality sessions max, rest E. Of 3-4 runs/week -> 1 quality session (beginners 0).
- By time, ~80-90% of weekly minutes in Zone 1 (warm-ups and recoveries count as Zone 1).
- Most common amateur error: easy days too fast (Zone 2 "grey zone"), leading to hard days too slow. Fix easy pace first.

## 3. Progressive overload
- Fitness improves only when load exceeds current capacity, then recovery allows adaptation. Increase ONE variable at a time: frequency -> duration -> intensity.
- Adaptation timelines (plan progressions around the slowest tissue):
  - Plasma volume: days to ~2 weeks.
  - Mitochondrial/aerobic enzymes: noticeable in 2-6 weeks.
  - Capillary density, VO2max: 6-12+ weeks.
  - Tendon collagen: turnover and stiffness change over months; tendon lags muscle. [Magnusson & Kjaer]
  - Bone remodelling: ~3-4 months per cycle; bone stress injuries follow fast load increases.
- Consequence: cardiovascular fitness grows faster than tendons/bone. Runners feel ready for more before their tissues are. Hold volume steady even when it "feels easy".

## 4. The 10 percent rule (use as a ceiling, not a target)
- Rule: do not increase weekly volume by more than ~10% per week.
- Evidence: a 13-week 10%-progression programme did NOT reduce injuries vs an 8-week faster programme in novices (20.8% vs 20.3% injured). [Buist 2008] Novices increasing weekly distance > 30% over 2 weeks had more distance-related injuries (patellofemoral pain, ITBS, etc.) than those < 10%. [Nielsen 2014]
- Newer evidence: single RUN distance matters more than weekly ratio. In 5,205 runners over 18 months, a single session > 10% longer than the longest run of the previous 30 days raised overuse-injury rate (dose-dependent: 10-30% spike, 30-100%, > 100% spike highest). Weekly ratio and ACWR did not show this. [Frandsen 2025]
- Apply as:
  1. Weekly volume: +5-10% per week for most runners; low-volume runners (< 20 km/week) may add up to ~2-3 km/week even if that is > 10%.
  2. Single run: never > 10% longer than the longest run in the last 30 days.
  3. Every 3rd or 4th week: down week at ~70-80% of previous week.
  4. Returning after a break or injury: restart below previous volume, rebuild; do not jump back.
- ACWR (acute 7-day / chronic 28-day load): 0.8-1.3 historically called "sweet spot", > 1.5 "danger zone" [Gabbett 2016]. Method is disputed statistically [Impellizzeri 2020]. Use only as a secondary warning signal, not as a rule.

## 5. Supercompensation and recovery
- Model: training stress -> fatigue (performance drops) -> recovery -> supercompensation (above baseline) -> next stimulus should land at or after this peak. If next stimulus comes too early repeatedly -> accumulated fatigue/overreaching; too late -> detraining.
- Practical recovery times (typical, trained runners):
  - Easy run: < 24 h.
  - Threshold/tempo: 24-48 h.
  - VO2max intervals: 48-72 h.
  - Long run (> 90 min): 48-72 h.
  - Race: marathon ~1 easy day per 1.6 km (1 mile) raced, i.e. no hard sessions for ~3-4 weeks; half ~5-7 easy days; 5K/10K 2-4 easy days (coaching rule of thumb, e.g. Pfitzinger). Same values in race-prep.md.
- Rule: never schedule two quality sessions on consecutive days (exception: advanced athletes in Hansons-style cumulative fatigue plans). Minimum 1 easy/rest day between quality sessions; 2 days between the long run and a hard session is preferable for amateurs.
- Signs of insufficient recovery (reduce load 1 week if 2+ present): resting HR ≥ 5-7 bpm above normal for several days, elevated RPE at usual paces, poor sleep, persistent soreness > 72 h, irritability, falling performance, frequent illness. Persistent (> 2-3 weeks) performance drop despite rest -> suspect overtraining/under-fuelling -> refer to doctor.
- Detraining: VO2max falls noticeably after ~2-4 weeks without training; ~4-14% after 4 weeks. [Mujika & Padilla 2000] Missing 1 week costs little.
- Sleep: 7-9 h; athletes benefit from the upper end.

## 6. Specificity
- The closer the race, the more training resembles the race (pace, terrain, duration, fuelling).
- Early: general aerobic + neuromuscular (E, strides, hills). Late: race-pace work (M pace for marathon, T/I for 5-10K).

## 7. Periodization phases
Four phases. Durations for a typical 12-24 week plan; scale down proportionally for shorter plans (minimum: 4 base, 3 build, 2 sharpen, 1-3 taper).

| Phase | Duration | Develops | Content | Quality sessions/week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 4-8 weeks (up to 12 for beginners or after a break) | aerobic capacity, capillaries, tendon/bone tolerance, running economy (strides) | mostly E, progressive long run, strides 4-8 x 20 s 2x/week, hill sprints/strides; volume grows here | 0-1 (strides/hills don't count as quality) |
| Build | 4-6 weeks | lactate threshold, VO2max, strength | T sessions, I sessions, hills; volume near peak | 1-2 |
| Sharpen (race-specific) | 3-6 weeks | race-pace efficiency, fuelling, pacing | race-pace work (M for marathon, T/I for 10K, I/R for 5K), long runs with M-pace segments, tune-up race 3-6 weeks out | 2 |
| Taper | 1-3 weeks (5K/10K 7-10 days, half 10-14 days, marathon 2-3 weeks) | freshness; fitness retained | volume -40-60%, keep intensity and frequency | 1-2 short |

Taper evidence: reduce volume 41-60% over ~2 weeks, keep intensity and frequency -> ~2-3% performance gain. [Bosquet 2007] Full detail in race-prep.md.

### Methodology references
- Daniels (24-week ideal): Phase I foundation (E + strides, 6 wk) -> II early quality (R pace) -> III transition quality (I pace, hardest phase) -> IV final quality (T pace, race specifics). Shorter plans cut from the front.
- Pfitzinger (marathon, 12/18 weeks): Mesocycle 1 endurance -> 2 lactate threshold + endurance -> 3 race preparation (VO2max + tune-up races) -> 4 taper and race (3 weeks). Hallmarks: medium-long runs midweek, long runs with the last part at M pace, LT tempo 20-40 min.
- Hansons (marathon, 18 weeks): cumulative fatigue; 6 run days/week; longest run ~26 km (16 mi) because it is run on tired legs; 3 SOS ("something of substance") days: speed (first ~10 weeks), strength (last ~8 weeks, M pace minus ~6 s/km), tempo at M pace (8-16 km).

## 8. Plan-generation rules (apply these)
1. Start from current weekly volume (average last 4 weeks), never from goal volume.
2. Long run: capped primarily by DURATION — ≤ 150 min (2.5 h) for most runners, up to 180 min (3 h) in marathon-and-longer plans. Time on feet, not distance, is what loads tendons and bones, so a slower runner reaches the cap at fewer kilometres, and reaching the cap counts as marathon-ready. Above ~50 km/week, ~30% of weekly volume is a guide for the long run's size [Daniels], not a hard cap: marathon builds may exceed it where the long run needs to reach its peak, but never the duration cap.
3. Recovery/down week every 3rd-4th week at 70-80% volume.
4. Quality sessions: 0 for beginners in first 8 weeks (strides only), 1 for 3-4 runs/week, 2 for 5-7 runs/week.
5. Hard/easy alternation. No quality session the day before or after the long run for amateurs.
6. Minimum plan lengths (from reasonable base): 5K 6-8 wk, 10K 8-10 wk, half 10-14 wk, marathon 16-20 wk (≥ 30-40 km/week base before starting a marathon plan).
7. Missed sessions: skip, do not stack. Missed ≥ 1 week due to illness/injury -> rebuild at reduced volume (see faq.md).
8. Strength training 2x/week (20-30 min, lower body + core) supports injury prevention and economy; keep heavy lifting away from the day before quality sessions.
