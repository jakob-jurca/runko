## Taper principles [Bosquet 2007; Mujika & Padilla 2003]
- Best evidence: taper ~8-14 days, reduce training VOLUME by 41-60%, keep INTENSITY (pace of quality work) and keep FREQUENCY (≥ ~80% of usual run days). Progressive/exponential reduction beats a single step cut.
- Expected benefit: ~2-3% performance improvement (range 0.5-6%).
- Do not cut intensity: short sharp race-pace work keeps fitness; fitness is not lost in 2-3 weeks of reduced volume.
- No new fitness can be gained in the final 10-14 days; extra hard work only adds fatigue.
- "Taper madness" (restlessness, phantom aches, feeling sluggish) is normal; reassure, do not add training.

## Taper by distance
| Distance | Taper length | Volume vs peak | Last hard session | Last long run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5K | 5-7 days | final week -30-40% | 4-5 days before: short I or R session (e.g. 4 x 400 R) | normal long run 7-10 days before, shortened |
| 10K | 7-10 days | final week -35-50% | 4-5 days before: e.g. 3-4 x 1 km at 10K pace | 8-10 days before, ~75% of usual |
| Half marathon | 10-14 days | week -2: -20-30%; race week -40-50% | 4-5 days before: e.g. 3 x 2 km at HM pace or 20 min T | 10-14 days before, ~16-18 km |
| Marathon | 2-3 weeks | week -3: -20-25%; week -2: -40%; race week -60% (Pfitzinger) | 7-10 days before: moderate M or T session; race week: short M-pace segments only (e.g. 3-5 km at M) | 3 weeks before (peak long run 29-35 km); 2 weeks before ~20-24 km |
- Hansons marathon taper is shorter (~10 days) because peak long runs are shorter (26 km). [Hansons]
- Beginners/first-timers: taper length at the upper end, volume cut at the upper end.

## Race week routine
- Maintain usual run days, shorter; include 4-6 strides 2-3 times; one short race-pace touch.
- Day before race: rest, or 15-20 min easy + 3-4 strides (most runners feel better with a short shakeout).
- Sleep: prioritise the 2-3 nights before; a poor final night before the race has little effect.
- Nutrition: marathon (and slower half runners) carb load 36-48 h (10-12 g/kg/day); others normal carb-rich diet. Details in nutrition.md.
- Logistics (list for the runner): collect bib early; check course map, elevation, aid stations (what drink/gel brand), start time, transport, bag drop, toilet locations; forecast; lay out kit the night before; pin bib; charge watch.
- Kit: only kit worn on long runs; anti-chafe on feet, thighs, underarms, nipples; race shoes tested ≥ 1-2 long runs/quality sessions.
- Avoid: new shoes, new foods, massage with deep tissue < 3-4 days before, long walks around expo, long travel on feet, hard strength training in the last 7-10 days.

## Race morning
- Wake ≥ 3 h before start. Breakfast 2-4 h before (1-4 g/kg carbs; familiar). Stop large fluid intake ~60-90 min before; sip to thirst.
- Caffeine if practised: ~60 min before (3 mg/kg). [nutrition.md]
- Arrive ≥ 60 min early. Toilet early (queues).
- Warm-up by distance:
  - 5K: 15-20 min easy + dynamic drills + 4-6 strides + 1-2 min at race pace, finish ≤ 10 min before start.
  - 10K: 10-15 min easy + 4 strides.
  - Half: 5-10 min easy jog + 2-3 strides, or walk + drills.
  - Marathon: minimal; 5 min walk/very easy jog, a few strides optional. First km acts as warm-up.
- Weather: cold -> throwaway layer; heat -> see adjustments below.

## Pacing strategy
- Best strategy for 5K-marathon: even pace or slight negative split (second half equal or up to ~1-2% faster). Most world records are run close to even. [Abbiss & Laursen 2008]
- Positive splits (fast start) are the most common amateur error; marathoners who start too fast slow down dramatically later; men slow more than women on average. [Smyth 2018]
- Goal pace source: current VDOT race prediction (vdot.md), NOT a wish. Marathon for first-timers and runners < 50 km/week: predicted time + 5-10%.
- By distance:
  - 5K: first km at goal pace (not faster, adrenaline tempts ~5-10 s/km fast); km 2-4 hold; last 1 km push.
  - 10K: first 2 km at goal pace or 2-3 s/km slower; hold 3-8; push final 2 km.
  - Half: first 5 km 3-5 s/km slower than goal; 5-16 km at goal; last 5 km increase if able.
  - Marathon: first 5-10 km 5-10 s/km slower than goal or exactly on goal; settle at goal pace to 30-32 km; the race "starts" at 30 km. Use effort check: at halfway it should feel controlled (RPE ~5-6).
- Hills: run even EFFORT, not even pace; let pace drop on uphills, do not sprint downhills.
- Wind: tuck behind runners in headwinds; groups help.
- Watch GPS can read 1-3% long in cities/tunnels/trees; use course km markers.
- Pace groups (pacers): useful if their target matches runner's VDOT prediction; start with the slower group if between two.

## Heat and conditions adjustments
- Marathon performance slows progressively as temperature rises above ~10-12 °C (WBGT); slower runners are affected more. [Ely 2007]
- Practical adjustments (approximate, coaching rules): 15-20 °C: slow goal by ~1-3%; 20-25 °C: ~3-6%; > 25 °C: 6%+, run by effort, consider changing goal to finishing safely. High humidity adds to these.
- Heat acclimatisation: 10-14 days of training in heat gives most adaptation (earlier improvements start after ~5-6 days). [Racinais 2015]
- In heat: pour water over head/neck, drink to thirst plus sodium; stop if dizzy, confused, stop sweating, or have chills -> medical tent.

## Common race-day mistakes
1. Going out too fast (most common and most costly).
2. Trying something new: shoes, gel, breakfast, caffeine dose, socks.
3. Setting goal pace from hope rather than recent training/race data.
4. Over-drinking in marathon (hyponatremia risk) or under-drinking in heat.
5. Skipping fuel in the marathon until feeling bad (start at 30-45 min).
6. Weaving in crowds (adds distance); standing in wrong start corral.
7. Too long/too hard warm-up for half/marathon; no warm-up for 5K.
8. Chasing the GPS instead of effort on hills.
9. Panicking about a slow kilometre; one slow km changes little, surging to recover wastes energy.
10. Racing the tune-up race or "testing" fitness in the taper.

## After the race
- Marathon: 1-2 weeks of rest or very easy running; no hard sessions for ~3-4 weeks (common rule of thumb: ~1 easy day per mile/1.6 km raced). [Pfitzinger]
- Half: 5-7 easy days before quality.
- 5K/10K: 2-4 easy days.
- Update VDOT from the result if conditions were normal (vdot.md).
- Next goal race: ≥ 8-12 weeks after a marathon for a full cycle.

## Tune-up races (in build/sharpen)
- Marathon plan: a half-marathon or 10K 3-6 weeks before (Pfitzinger); do not race > 21 km in the last 3 weeks.
- Half plan: a 5K-10K 3-4 weeks before.
- Use results to confirm or adjust goal pace.
