Paces refer to VDOT zones in vdot.md (E, M, T, I, R). Effort scale RPE 1-10. Volume caps per session are Daniels' limits unless stated.
Each `## ` heading is a plan engine `type` value. Variants that the engine files under that type are `### ` subsections.
Quality sessions = tempo, interval, repetition types (and M-pace runs). Strides and easy hill sprints do not count as quality.

**Weekly structure templates**
- 3 runs/week: 1 quality + 1 easy + 1 long (beginners: 3 easy, one slightly longer).
- 4 runs/week: 1 quality + 2 easy + 1 long.
- 5 runs/week: 2 quality + 2 easy + 1 long (or 1 quality + long with M segment).
- 6-7 runs/week: 2 quality + long + 3-4 easy/recovery (Hansons: 3 SOS days incl. long).
- Order example (5 runs): Tue quality, Wed easy, Thu quality, Fri rest, Sat easy, Sun long, Mon rest.

**Session structure (any quality session):** Warm-up 10-20 min E + 4 strides -> main set -> cool-down 10 min E. Warm-up and cool-down count toward weekly volume.

## easy
- Purpose: aerobic base (mitochondria, capillaries, cardiac stroke volume), fat oxidation, tissue adaptation, active recovery. Builds most of total volume. [Daniels; Seiler 2010]
- Structure: 30-75 min continuous (beginners 20-30 min). Optional 4-8 x 20 s strides at the end.
- Target: E pace; 65-79% HRmax; RPE 2-4; can speak full sentences.
- Frequency: all non-quality days; ~70-85% of weekly volume.
- Phase: all phases.
- Common error: running too fast. If breathing prevents full sentences, slow down or walk.
- Plain language: "Relaxed, chatty pace. This is where most of your fitness is built, so slower is fine."

### Recovery run
- Purpose: add volume or blood flow without adding stress; used day after quality/long run.
- Structure: 20-45 min very easy. Optional for runners doing < 5 runs/week (rest day is equivalent or better).
- Target: slow end of E range or slower; < 70% HRmax; RPE 1-3.
- Frequency: 0-3/week, only for runners doing 5+ runs/week.
- Phase: all.
- Common error: turning it into a normal/moderate run. Pace does not matter here.
- Plain language: "Very gentle, shake out the legs from yesterday. Pace doesn't matter today."

### Strides
- Purpose: economy and form, leg speed, low fatigue. Not counted as a quality session.
- Structure: 4-8 x 15-25 s accelerating to ~R pace, full walk/jog recovery 45-60 s. After easy runs.
- Frequency: 1-3/week, all phases, including beginners after ~4 weeks of continuous running.
- Plain language: "A few short, smooth pickups to wake the legs up. Fast but relaxed, never a sprint."

## long
- Purpose: endurance, glycogen storage, fat use, musculoskeletal durability, mental tolerance. Marathon-critical.
- Structure: continuous E pace. Length capped by duration: ≤ 150 min, up to 180 min in marathon plans; below ~50 km/week the long run is at most 60% of the week on 2 run days, 45% on 3, and 45% on 4+ days under 40 km (36% from 40 km); above ~50 km/week ~36% is a guide, not a cap (see methodology.md). Marathon plans peak at ~29-32 km or 3 h, whichever comes first (Pfitzinger), or 26 km (Hansons, run on pre-fatigued legs). Half-marathon plans peak at 16-22 km or 2.5 h. 5K/10K: 60-90 min.
- Variants (build/sharpen, experienced runners only): last 20-30% at M pace; M-pace segments (e.g. 3 x 5 km M within 25-30 km); fast-finish.
- Target: E pace; M-pace segments per vdot.md.
- Frequency: 1/week (every 2nd week for low-volume runners in down weeks).
- Phase: base (grows), build, sharpen (peak 3-5 weeks before race), taper (shortened).
- Increase: max 10% longer than longest run in prior 30 days (see methodology.md). Fuel > 75-90 min (see nutrition.md).
- Common error: running it too fast, racing it, skipping fuel.
- Plain language: "Your weekly endurance run. Steady and easy; the goal is time on your feet, not speed."

## tempo
- Purpose: raise lactate threshold = pace sustainable before lactate accumulates; key predictor for 10K-marathon.
- Structure: 10-15 min warm-up, 20 min continuous at T (Daniels), or 20-40 min at LT pace (Pfitzinger: ~15K-half-marathon race pace), 10 min cool-down.
- Target: T pace; 88-92% HRmax; RPE 6-7; "comfortably hard", short phrases only.
- Frequency: 1/week (the quality session for most amateurs).
- Phase: late base (short), build, sharpen.
- Common error: running at 10K race effort. T is a controlled effort sustainable for ~60 min in a race.
- Plain language: "Comfortably hard: you could hold it for about an hour in a race, and you can only speak in short phrases."

### Threshold intervals
- Purpose: same as tempo, more total time at T with less strain.
- Structure: 3-6 x 5-8 min (or 1-2 km reps, or 2 x 15 min) at T with 1 min jog per 5 min run. Total T ≤ 10% of weekly volume. Examples: 5 x 1 km T / 1 min; 3 x 2 km T / 2 min; 4 x 8 min T / 90 s.
- Target: T pace. Recovery short on purpose.
- Frequency: 1/week, alternate with tempo.
- Phase: build, sharpen.
- Plain language: "Tempo effort broken into chunks with short jogs, so you get more time at that pace."

### Marathon-pace run
- Purpose: race-specific endurance and fuel strategy at goal pace.
- Structure: 8-16 km at M after 2-3 km warm-up (Hansons "tempo"); or inside long run. Cap: ≤ 20% of weekly volume or 29 km.
- Target: M pace; 80-90% HRmax; RPE 5-6.
- Frequency: 1/week in marathon build/sharpen.
- Phase: build, sharpen (marathon and half only).
- Plain language: "Practise your goal race pace so it feels familiar and controlled on race day."

### Progression run
- Purpose: teaches pacing and running fast on tired legs; moderate stress.
- Structure: 40-75 min: start at slow E, finish the last 10-20 min at M or T. Or thirds: E -> M -> T.
- Target: RPE rises from 3 to 6-7.
- Frequency: 0-1/week (replaces a quality session or a long run variant).
- Phase: base (gentle), build, sharpen.
- Plain language: "Start easy and finish strong; each part a little quicker than the last."

## interval
- Purpose: maximise time near VO2max -> raise aerobic ceiling. Reps of 3-5 min allow ~2 min at VO2max each. [Daniels; Midgley 2007; Billat 2001]
- Structure: 5-6 x 1000 m, 4-5 x 1200 m, 5 x 3 min, 6 x 800 m. Recovery: jog equal to or slightly less than rep time. Total at I ≤ 8% of weekly volume or 10 km. Never > 5 min per rep at I pace.
- Target: I pace (~3-5K race pace); 95-100% HRmax reached late in each rep; RPE 8-9.
- Frequency: 1/week max, 0 for beginners (< 3 months) and in base.
- Phase: build, sharpen (5K/10K: key session; marathon: build and early sharpen).
- Common error: first reps too fast, fading later. All reps equal pace. If pace cannot be held, stop the session.
- Plain language: "Hard efforts of a few minutes at about 5K race pace, with jog recoveries. Keep every rep the same pace."

### Fartlek
- Purpose: unstructured speed play; aerobic + speed with low mental pressure. Good intro to quality work.
- Structure: 30-45 min with e.g. 6-10 x 1 min moderate-hard / 1-2 min easy, or landmark-based surges.
- Target: surges at T to I effort (RPE 6-8), recoveries at E.
- Frequency: 1/week as the quality session, or instead of intervals.
- Phase: base (gentle), build.
- Plain language: "Speed play: surge for a minute, float for a minute or two, by feel."

### Hill repeats
- Hill repeats (strength + VO2max): 6-10 x 60-90 s at 5K effort (RPE 7-8) on 4-6% grade, jog down recovery. Phase: base end / build. Counts as interval.
- Long hills (strength-endurance): 4-6 x 2-3 min at T-to-I effort. Phase: build.
- Use effort, not pace. Caution for Achilles/calf issues (see injuries.md): steep uphills load Achilles; downhill running loads quads and knees.
- Plain language: "Strong efforts uphill, easy jog back down. Run by effort, not pace."

## repetition
- Purpose: running economy, speed, mechanics, neuromuscular recruitment; little aerobic stress. [Daniels; Barnes & Kilding 2015]
- Structure: 8-12 x 200 m or 6-8 x 400 m, or 200-200-400 sets. Full recovery (walk/jog 2-3x rep time). Total ≤ 5% weekly volume or 8 km.
- Target: R pace (~mile race pace); fast, relaxed, good form; not sprinting.
- Frequency: 1/week in phases where R is emphasised.
- Phase: late base / early build (Daniels phase II), sharpen for 5K/1500 m.
- Common error: short recovery (turns it into intervals).
- Plain language: "Short, fast and relaxed with full recovery. The point is good form at speed, not suffering."

### Short hill sprints
- Short hill sprints (strength, power, tendon stiffness): 6-10 x 8-12 s maximal on steep (6-10%) hill, walk back full recovery (~90 s). Phase: base, also year-round. Low injury risk for most. Frequency 1/week. Counts as repetition-type.
- Plain language: "Very short, powerful uphill bursts with a full walk-back rest. Builds strength and spring."

## cross
- Purpose: aerobic stimulus without impact; used for injury, extra volume for injury-prone runners, recovery.
- Options ranked by running specificity: deep-water running > elliptical > cycling > swimming/rowing. Time equivalence roughly 1 min running ≈ 1-1.5 min cross-training at similar HR (approximate).
- Frequency: 0-2/week; replaces runs when injured (see injuries.md).
- Plain language: "Aerobic work without the pounding: keeps your fitness while your legs recover."

## rest
- Purpose: full recovery, adaptation, injury prevention.
- Frequency: beginners 2-4/week, intermediate 1-2/week, advanced 0-1/week.
- Place after the long run or hardest session.
- Plain language: "A real day off. Adaptation happens while you rest."

## race
- Purpose: goal event, fitness test (VDOT update), tune-up practice.
- Tune-up race 3-6 weeks before a goal marathon or half (Pfitzinger): 10K-half distance.
- Recovery after race: see methodology.md section 5.
- Plain language: "Race day or a time trial: give it an honest effort and pace it evenly."
